# Physics Methodology

Detailed description of the volcanological models implemented in
`src/physics.py` and used by the simulation modules, with literature
references and the assumptions/limitations of each.

---

## 1. Atmosphere

An isothermal exponential atmosphere is used for density:

```
ρ(z) = ρ₀ · exp(−z / H)
```

with ρ₀ = 1.225 kg/m³ and scale height H = 8500 m. This is adequate for the
troposphere/lower stratosphere where eruption columns and ash transport
occur, and feeds both the ballistic drag and ash settling calculations.

---

## 2. Plume Height ↔ Mass Eruption Rate

The single most-used empirical relationship in ash-cloud forecasting is the
Mastin et al. (2009) power law between observable plume height H (km above
vent) and mass eruption rate MER (kg/s):

```
H = 2.00 · MER^0.241
```

We implement both the forward form (`plume_height_from_mer`) and its
inversion (`mass_eruption_rate_from_height`). The relation is calibrated on
dozens of historical eruptions and is the operational standard at the Volcanic
Ash Advisory Centres.

**Assumption:** dry, vertically-rising column in a still atmosphere; strong
crosswinds bend the plume and reduce effective height (not modelled here).

---

## 3. Eruption Column Dynamics

The column is split into two regimes (`PlumeModel`):

- **Gas-thrust (jet) region** — momentum-driven, near the vent, decelerating
  as the dense jet entrains air. Height scale ≈ a few vent radii.
- **Convective region** — once entrained air is heated and the mixture
  becomes buoyant, the column rises convectively, decelerating toward the
  neutral-buoyancy (umbrella) level near the empirical plume height.

Radius grows with height (`r ≈ r_vent + 0.10 z`) representing turbulent
entrainment — the classic ~0.1 entrainment-coefficient assumption (Morton,
Taylor & Turner 1956).

**Reference:** Sparks et al. (1997), *Volcanic Plumes*, Wiley.

---

## 4. Pyroclastic Density Currents (PDCs)

PDCs are modelled with the **energy-cone (energy-line) method** of Malin &
Sheridan (1982), the standard first-order tool for PDC hazard zonation.

A pyroclastic flow generated by column collapse behaves as if its centre of
mass slides down an "energy line" of constant slope H/L (the **Heim
coefficient**) from the collapse height. The flow stops where this energy
line intersects the topography. For flat ground:

```
L_max = H_collapse / (H/L)
```

Typical Heim coefficients:

| Mobility | H/L | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Very mobile (large volume) | 0.10–0.18 | Pinatubo 1991 |
| Mobile | 0.18–0.25 | Vesuvius 79 |
| Moderate | 0.25–0.35 | smaller dome-collapse flows |

In the dynamic simulation, individual PDC particles additionally:

- accelerate downslope under the terrain gradient (`−∇h · g`),
- are opposed by Coulomb friction (`μ ≈ H/L`),
- are clamped to physically realistic front speeds (≤ 120 m/s),
- freeze where the local terrain rises above the energy line.

This reproduces both the **runout distance** (energy line) and the
**topographic channelling** of flows into valleys — the two features that
matter most for hazard mapping.

**References:** Malin & Sheridan (1982); Sheridan & Malin (1983);
Branney & Kokelaar (2002), *Pyroclastic Density Currents*.

---

## 5. Ballistic Ejecta

Blocks and bombs follow Newtonian projectile motion with quadratic
aerodynamic drag:

```
a = g + a_drag
a_drag = −½ Cd ρ_air A |v| v / m
```

with per-clast diameter (0.15–0.8 m), drag coefficient Cd ≈ 1 (rough
sphere), and rock density 2500 kg/m³. Launch velocities (200–320 m/s) and
high launch angles (55–88°) produce the proximal ballistic field. Drag is
altitude-dependent through ρ_air(z).

**Result:** impact ranges of ~0.1–2.5 km for the default scenario, matching
documented proximal ballistic hazard areas.

---

## 6. Ash Settling & Dispersal

Each tephra particle is advected horizontally by the mean wind and falls at
its **terminal settling velocity**, computed by Reynolds-regime selection:

- **Stokes' law** (Re < 1, fine ash):
  `v_t = (ρ_p − ρ_air) g d² / (18 μ)`
- **Turbulent drag** (Re ≥ 1, coarser clasts):
  `v_t = √(4 (ρ_p − ρ_air) g d / (3 Cd ρ_air))`

Particles deposit onto a 64×64 ground-loading grid at their landing cell,
accumulating mass (kg/m²) — the raw material for an **isopach map**
(lines of equal tephra thickness), the standard ashfall hazard product.

**Reference:** Bonadonna et al. (1998); Pfeiffer et al. (2005) (TEPHRA model).

---

## 7. Volcanic Explosivity Index (VEI)

`VEI_TABLE` encodes the Newhall & Self (1982) classification (erupted
volume, typical plume height, descriptive class) for VEI 0–7, used to label
scenarios and sanity-check plume heights against erupted volume.

---

## 8. Summary of Assumptions

| Model | Key simplifying assumption |
|---|---|
| Atmosphere | Isothermal, still air (no layered wind) |
| Plume | Axisymmetric, no wind bending |
| PDC | Depth-averaged kinematic energy cone, single Heim coefficient |
| Ballistics | Spherical clasts, constant Cd |
| Ashfall | Single mean wind, no aggregation |

These are the standard first-order assumptions used in rapid hazard
assessment. Each is noted in the Technical Report as a candidate for
refinement (full CFD PDC solver, layered-wind ash model, real DEM import).
